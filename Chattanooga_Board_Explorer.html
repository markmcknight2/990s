These changes exist only in this browser.

Cohort and sector edits made here are stored in this browser's local storage. They do not reach Organization_Registry.xlsx, the spreadsheet, or anything generated from the data — and they are lost if you clear site data or open this dashboard on another machine.

To keep them: click Export JSON, which saves classification_assignments.json. Put that file in your 990 Research Assistant folder and ask Claude to merge it into the registry. The registry is what every report is built from.

Cohorts were seeded from each organization's existing cohort plus verified ANCA membership cross-referenced from ANCA_Members_Tracker.xlsx; sectors were seeded from the single sector each org already carried. Because an organization can hold several cohorts and several sectors, the per-column counts add up to more than the organizations tracked — that is intentional. Data shows whether an org has board rosters (B) and financials (F) on file. Primary sector is the first sector ticked, reading left to right; it drives single-colour bar charts and the “cross-sector connectors” count on the People tab, so reordering matters there. The Avg Board Size by Sector chart and the sector filter use all of an org's sectors.

About this dashboard: A queryable cross-sector board database for Chattanooga-area nonprofits, with plans to expand to national peer organizations. Data extracted from IRS 990, 990-EZ, and 990-PF filings via ProPublica. Fiscal years FY2020–FY2024 are generally covered; some organizations' most recent filing is FY2025.

BE-001OpenLowCoverageTrees Atlanta has 5 of 7 filing years
The IRS index lists FY2018, 2019, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025. FY2019 was recovered by the BE-007 fix, leaving FY2018 and FY2022 listed by the IRS but absent from every published bulk archive. There is no retained legacy row to fall back on because Trees Atlanta was added after the legacy snapshot was frozen.
Next: Nothing to do locally. Re-check after a future IRS archive release, or source those two years from Candid.
2026-07-28
BE-002OpenMediumReconciliationErlanger Foundation FY2024 appears twice
Erlanger changed its fiscal year end. FY2023 closes 2023-06-30 and the next return closes 2024-11-30, so by the period-end convention both fall in FY2024 — a 5-month short period alongside the full prior year. The archive holds the short stub (revenue 1,400,817) while the retained legacy row holds the full year (4,155,547). Both are correct for their period. This drives all four material conflicts in merged/reconciliation.csv.
Next: Decide which period the workbook and dashboard should display for FY2024, or show both with the period end. Any join on (EIN, fiscal year) alone will mishandle this org.
2026-07-28
BE-006OpenLowWorkflowDashboard cohort edits stay in the browser until exported
Reassigning cohorts or sectors in the Organization Index writes only to this browser's localStorage. Nothing reaches Organization_Registry.xlsx until the taxonomy is exported and folded back in with apply_dashboard_export.py. A warning banner now makes this visible, but the round trip is still manual.
Next: Acceptable for now. Would only be solved properly by making the dashboard write to a file, which a static HTML page cannot do.
2026-07-28
BE-008OpenHighVerificationNo automated check that every listed filing actually loaded
BE-007 was a silent failure - no error, no warning, the filing simply was not there. It only surfaced because someone asked about one organization's history by name. Nothing currently compares what the IRS indexes list against what the database actually holds, so a whole class of quiet extraction losses would go unnoticed. The pieces already exist: the indexes give the expected filings per EIN, and the filings table gives the actual.
Next: Build a per-organization coverage report - expected vs loaded per fiscal year, with a reason for each gap (not published in any archive / archive not downloaded / decode failure). Run it after every build and fail loudly on unexplained gaps.
2026-07-28
BE-003AcceptedMediumCoverageIRS bulk archive is sparse for submission years 2021-2022
The IRS publishes only one bulk archive for each of 2021 and 2022, against 12-16 for every other year, so the local archive genuinely cannot cover FY2020-2022 for many organizations. 42 org-years in merged/financials.csv are retained legacy figures for exactly this reason and are marked 'retained (pre-archive)'.
Next: Upstream limitation, not fixable locally. Never delete legacy rows to 'clean up' these gaps.
2026-07-28
BE-004AcceptedLowData quality990-PF figures differ ~0.3% from the legacy source
For foundations the archive reports Part I column (a) revenue and expenses per books, traceable to a line on the form. The legacy figures came from the IRS Statistics of Income extract, which applies normalizations that cannot be reproduced from the filing — for Maclellan FY2021 the gap is a 95,623 capital-gain line on revenue and a composite 84,296 on expenses. Affects 33 filings across 9 foundations.
Next: Accepted. Column (a) is the defensible choice because a reviewer can point at it on the form.
2026-07-28
BE-005AcceptedLowCoverageNorth Chickamauga Creek Conservancy has no financial or board data
Gross receipts under $50k means the organization files Form 990-N, the e-Postcard, which carries no financial data and no Part VII. Confirmed present in the IRS 990-N database for tax years 2014-2018 and 2020-2024. This is a small filer, not a delinquent one.
Next: No action. Report as 'small filer, 990-N on file' rather than as missing data.
2026-07-28
BE-007ResolvedHighExtractionFilings were dropped when the index year and archive year differed
For 2019-2023 the IRS index publishes no XML_BATCH_ID, so those filings are located by scanning archives. The scan only looked in archives whose name matched the index year - but a return listed in index_2020 is often bundled in a 2019 archive. Atlanta Botanical Garden FY2018 is the worked example: listed in index_2020, stored in download990xml_2019_3.zip, never loaded. Silent - the filing simply did not appear.
Next: Fixed 2026-07-28: the scan now covers every archive, with a cached entry-location map so it stays fast. Rebuild the database to recover affected filings.
